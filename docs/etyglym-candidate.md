# ETyGLyM™: Substratic Etymological and Glyphic Inquiry

## 1. What Is ETyGLyM™?

ETyGLyM™ is an emerging utility specification for mapping **etyms** (lexical roots, linguistic origins, and morphological history) and **glyphs** (inscriptions, marks, visual forms, and character identities).

It approaches language and symbols through two distinct, equally legitimate modes of participation:

- **Officially:** Inspecting documented historical corpora, language registries, epigraphic attestations, and scholarly limits.
- **Playfully:** Constructing, partitioning, and examining coined words, experimental marks, and structural seams without requiring prior standardization or historical attestation.

Rather than forcing a premature choice between historical rigor and creative invention, ETyGLyM™ holds both in clear view while keeping their evidential standing strictly separated.

## 2. Core Operational Architecture — Public Design Abstraction

The proposed utility distinguishes two analytical capacities. This is a **conceptual description**, not a disclosure of an execution engine.

```text
┌────────────────────────┐         ┌────────────────────────┐
│       ETYM LANE        │         │       GLYM LANE        │
│  Lexical, Historical,  │ ◄─────► │ Graphic, Inscriptional │
│ Morphological Inquiry  │    y    │ and Character Inquiry  │
└────────────────────────┘  Hinge  └────────────────────────┘
```

- **ETYM (Lexical Inquiry):** Investigates attested roots, prefixes, historical borrowings, semantic shifts, and reconstructed forms. Modern surface resemblance is never assumed to establish genealogical descent.
- **GLYM (Inscriptional Inquiry):** Investigates physical and digital marks, script families, graphemes, and substrate traces. A mark can be examined on its own terms without demanding an established linguistic root.
- **Typographic Hinge Invariant (`y`):** The exact capitalization **ETyGLyM™** preserves the lowercase `y` as a bilateral bridge (`ETy ↔ GLyM`). The design invokes historical orthographic borrowing (*i Graeca*) as a naming reference. The hinge retains the distinction between lexical meaning and the physical mark; neither is allowed to erase the other.

## 3. The Substratic Stance

ETyGLyM™ is declared **relationally substratic to STD 3**. This is an architectural orientation concerning the separation of carriage and inquiry, **not** a claim of implementation, certification, or compliance with Internet host requirements in RFC 1122 / RFC 1123.

- **Carriage Separation:** Physical transport, network pipelines, and storage media are treated as carriers (*Via*). Mechanical storage constraints, including buffer ceilings and field delimiters, do not by themselves establish the validity or meaning of a carried payload.
- **Standardization ≠ Erasure of Difference:** When a mark encounters an interface constraint, such as a fixed-width buffer or non-ASCII host-label rule, the proposed approach does not silently sanitize or truncate it. An incompatibility is to be retained and recorded as *vestigium* (diagnostic remainder data).

These are candidate design commitments, not claims about an operating system.

## 4. Descriptive Record States

The proposed record model distinguishes five **non-hierarchical** states to prevent unearned assumptions from entering the inquiry ledger.

| Record State | Function & Scope | Epistemic Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Attestation** | Documents what a historical source, corpus, or registry explicitly shows. | Recording a citation ≠ independent verification. |
| **Invention** | Preserves creative coinages, neologisms, and novel graphic marks. | Creative construction ≠ historical root. |
| **Interpretation** | Details candidate readings, models, or semantic trajectories. | Reading an interface ≠ establishing causality. |
| **Comparison** | Examines resonance, structural tension, or formal resemblance across signs. | Resemblance ≠ historical descent. |
| **Standards Check** | Evaluates how a token interacts with declared technical protocols (e.g., Unicode, UTF-8, STD 3). | Machine compatibility ≠ semantic truth. |
